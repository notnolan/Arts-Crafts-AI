I'm interested in learning more about the arts and crafts movement. Particularly, I'm approaching this project with two hypotheses:

**H1: Craft in the arts and craft movement focused on personal development, or the ability to develop practice as well as technical skill.**

- **John Ruskin, "The Nature of Gothic"** (_The Stones of Venice_, vol. 2, 1853). 
	- 
- **William Morris, "Useful Work versus Useless Toil"** (1884) and **"The Lesser Arts"** (1877). Both address the relationship between making and the maker's development.
- **William Morris, "The Aims of Art"** (1886) and **"The Revival of Handicraft"** (_Fortnightly Review_, 1888). The latter is the most direct statement on handicraft as a practice.
- **John Ruskin, _The Elements of Drawing_** (1857) and _**The Seven Lamps of Architecture**_ (1849). The first is Ruskin's pedagogy of training perception and hand together. "The Lamp of Life" in the second concerns the worker's vitality in the work.
- **T. J. Cobden-Sanderson, "The Arts and Crafts Movement"** (1905). Written by the person credited with coining the movement's name, and explicitly about the maker's inner life and aims.
- **C. R. Ashbee, _Craftsmanship in Competitive Industry_** (1908) and _**An Endeavour Towards the Teaching of John Ruskin and William Morris**_ (1901). Practical accounts of the Guild of Handicraft as a place of training and personal formation.
- _**The Craftsman**_ (Gustav Stickley, 1901–1916). A serial, so check the digitized runs for pieces on craft training.
- **Frank Lloyd Wright, "The Art and Craft of the Machine"** (1901).

**H2: The craft movement made a distinction between craft on a technical level and craft on a theory or practice based level.**

- **John Ruskin, "The Nature of Gothic"** (_The Stones of Venice_, vol. 2, 1853)
- **William Morris, "Useful Work versus Useless Toil"** (1884) and **"The Lesser Arts"** (1877). Both address the relationship between making and the maker's development.
- **William Morris, "The Aims of Art"** (1886) and **"The Revival of Handicraft"** (_Fortnightly Review_, 1888). The latter is the most direct statement on handicraft as a practice.
- **W. R. Lethaby, _Art and Workmanship_** (1913 essay, reprinted in _Form in Civilization_, 1922) and his writings from his Central School of Arts and Crafts years (principal from 1896). Lethaby is the most consistent voice on how design, workmanship, and teaching relate.
- **C. R. Ashbee, _Should We Stop Teaching Art?_** (1911). It deals with art education and the split between design and execution.
- _**Arts and Crafts Essays**_ (1893, by members of the Arts and Crafts Exhibition Society, preface by Morris). Contributors address craft categories and the relationship between designer and maker.
- **Walter Crane, _The Claims of Decorative Art_** (1892) and _**William Morris to Whistler**_ (1911). Crane discusses design vs. execution within the movement's institutions.
- **Edward Johnston, _Writing & Illuminating, & Lettering_** (1906).

### Secondary scholarship

- **Peter Betjemann, _Talking Shop: The Language of Craft in an Age of Consumption_** (2011). 
- **Eileen Boris, _Art and Labor: Ruskin, Morris, and the Craftsman Ideal in America_** (1986).
- **Alan Crawford, _C. R. Ashbee: Architect, Designer & Romantic Socialist_** (1985). The best source on the Guild of Handicraft's training and pedagogy.
- **Elizabeth Cumming and Wendy Kaplan, _The Arts and Crafts Movement_** (1991), **Rosalind Blakesley, _The Arts and Crafts Movement_** (2006), and **Gillian Naylor, _The Arts and Crafts Movement_** (1971). General surveys with bibliographies to mine.
- **Anthea Callen, _Angel in the Studio: Women in the Arts and Crafts Movement_** (1979). It covers training and workshop practice for women makers.
